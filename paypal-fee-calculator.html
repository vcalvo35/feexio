How PayPal Fees Work in 2026

PayPal does not have a single "standard" fee — the rate depends on how the payment is sent. For US businesses and freelancers, the most common rate is 3.49% + $0.49, which applies to PayPal Checkout on a website and to PayPal invoices and payment links. The calculator above uses that rate; the table below shows the surcharges that apply to international and converted payments.

Payment Type Rate (US)
PayPal Checkout (website / API integration) 3.49% + $0.49
Standard invoice & payment links 3.49% + $0.49
International (cross-border) + 1.5% additional
Currency conversion + 3–4% spread

Last verified: November 2026 — Always confirm at paypal.com/merchant-fees

For a $100 PayPal Checkout or invoice payment: $100 × 3.49% = $3.49 + $0.49 = $3.98 fee, netting $96.02. If the buyer is outside the US, the 1.5% international fee raises the fee on the same payment to $5.48.

How to Price Your Work to Cover PayPal Fees

To receive a specific net amount after PayPal fees, add the fixed fee to your target and divide by one minus the percentage: (target + $0.49) ÷ (1 − 0.0349). For example, to receive exactly $100.00: $100.49 ÷ 0.9651 = $104.12 gross invoice amount.

Many freelancers and sellers add a flat 3–4% to their prices to offset PayPal fees, or include a line item in their invoices that reads "Payment processing fee." Always check PayPal's official rates for your country — rates vary internationally.

PayPal Friends & Family vs Goods & Services

PayPal Friends & Family (F&F) transfers have no fee for the sender or receiver — but they provide zero buyer or seller protection. Using F&F for business transactions violates PayPal's terms of service and can result in account suspension. Always use Goods & Services for business payments, even if it means paying the fee. How Much to Invoice to Receive an Exact Amount

To receive a specific net amount, add the fixed fee and divide by one minus the percentage: (target + $0.49) ÷ (1 − 0.0349). To receive exactly $500: ($500 + $0.49) ÷ 0.9651 = $518.59. Check it: $518.59 × 3.49% + $0.49 = $18.59 in fees, leaving $500.00.

If the client pays from another country, add the 1.5% international fee to the percentage (4.99%), and if they pay in a different currency, expect a conversion spread on top. In those cases a bank transfer or a service such as Stripe may cost less – compare with the Stripe fee calculator.

Common mistakes

  • Adding 3.49% to your price, which still leaves you short because the fee is calculated on the higher total.
  • Using Friends & Family for business payments to avoid fees – it removes seller protection and breaks PayPal's rules.
  • Assuming every PayPal payment has the same rate. Rates vary by payment type and country; always confirm on PayPal's merchant fees page.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the PayPal fee for a $100 payment?

In the US, PayPal Checkout and invoice payments cost 3.49% + $0.49. On a $100 payment the fee is $3.98 ($3.49 + $0.49), leaving you with $96.02. Other payment types have different rates, so check PayPal's merchant fees page for yours.

How do I avoid PayPal fees?

You can't eliminate PayPal fees on business payments, but you can offset them by adding 3-4% to your invoice total, or using alternative payment methods like bank transfer for large transactions.

Are PayPal fees different for international payments?

Yes. International payments add a currency conversion fee of 3-4% on top of the standard transaction fee. The total cost for an international payment can reach 6-8% of the transaction amount.

Does PayPal charge fees for receiving money from friends?

Friends & Family (F&F) transfers are free within the same country when funded from a PayPal balance or bank account. However, F&F is prohibited for business transactions and provides no buyer or seller protection.
